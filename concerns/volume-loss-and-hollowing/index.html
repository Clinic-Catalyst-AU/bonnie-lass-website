The face that deflated

Not lined. Just empty.

Some faces age by wrinkling. Others age by deflating - and the second one is far harder to name, which is why so many people never get it properly treated.

Faces lose structure before they lose smoothness

The fat pads that hold up the mid-face shrink and slide with age. The cheeks flatten, shadows appear under the eyes, the jawline blurs and the corners of the mouth turn down - all without a single new wrinkle. You look tired in photographs and you cannot work out why.

Why the mirror lies to you

You will focus on the hollow you can see - usually under the eyes. But tear troughs are very often caused by the cheek above them having dropped, not by the trough itself. Treat the trough and you can make it worse. This is exactly the kind of mistake that a full facial assessment prevents.

What we would actually do

Your nurse assesses the whole face and its structure before treating any single part of it. Volume is restored where it was genuinely lost, in the order that supports the face - which is often not the place you would have pointed at.

The restraint matters more than the product

Overfilled faces are not the result of too much product so much as product in the wrong places. Our stated bias is towards natural enhancement: we would rather do less and have you come back than do too much and have you regret it.

The questions people actually ask

Straight answers

Will I end up looking overfilled?

Not if the assessment is done properly and the restraint is real. This is the single most common fear people bring in, and it is a reasonable one.

Can this be reversed if I hate it?

Some volumising treatments can be dissolved. That is a conversation to have at consultation, before anything is done, and your nurse will have it with you.

Is this the same as a facelift?

No. These are non-surgical treatments. There is a point at which surgery is genuinely the better answer, and your nurse will tell you when you have reached it rather than sell you something that will not deliver.

How long does it last?

It depends on the treatment and on you. Your nurse will give you a realistic range rather than a best case.
